# Volume 4 — *The Fourth Son at Court* (v3 active outline)
> **Status:** planning draft; refine before Arena drafting.
> **Mode:** courtly caper and social aura fantasy. Casian chooses the game; the court is not another invading army.

## Volume promise

After three volumes of claiming and securing the eastern marches, Casian wants one thing the Compact cannot quietly take: a royal charter that makes its roads and protections difficult to challenge for a generation. He goes to court because it sounds entertaining, because Seraine is there, and because he wants to see whether the kingdom can be made useful.

The capital expects a border baron and his irrelevant fourth son. Instead it gets a smiling boy who turns a season of gossip, masquerades, forged accounts, secret societies, and noble ambition into a game nobody realizes he is winning.

## Casian movement

Casian begins as a man who acts from the garden through the Hollow. He ends the volume knowing he enjoys being present in a larger game: speaking, flirting, provoking, and choosing people as allies. He does not want a throne; he discovers that he likes shaping a world that is awake enough to play back.

## End state

Edric receives an Eastern March Charter through a spectacular court defeat of Duke Lucan's faction. Lucan lives, disgraced and politically neutralized, rather than becoming another erased villain. The Crown understands that Vale is a partner to court, not a small house to command. Seraine and Casian move from implication to an acknowledged but undefined romantic possibility.

## New drafting names

- **Lucan** — Duke of Redmere; elegant, competitive court operator who assumes every house has a price.
- **Caedmon** — Royal Chancellor; observant, dry, loyal to the Crown rather than a faction.
- **Orlan** — Royal Marshal; blunt soldier who believes the eastern Compact must be measured before it becomes a danger.

| 1 | A Charter for a Garden / Casian–Edric | Casian casually proposes going to court for a charter. Edric thinks it is a sensible family duty; Casian thinks it will be fun. |
| 2 | The Road West / Family | The Vale family travels together. Casian gets to be the unimportant son amid arguments, food, and Brennan's terrible poetry. |
| 3 | The Capital's Smile / Lucan | Lucan sees the Compact's wealth and decides to acquire its charter, its road rights, and perhaps Seraine's attention. |

| 5 | The Marshal's Inspection / Orlan | Orlan asks harmless questions about Marches defenses. Casian answers honestly enough to leave the Marshal more confused than before. |
| 6 | The Fourth Son's Errand / Casian | Sent to buy ribbon for Rowan, Casian wanders the capital and quietly makes half the servants' network like him. |
| 7 | A Ball With Too Many Knives / Seraine | At the first ball, Lucan tests Casian with humiliation and flirtation. Casian lets everyone laugh at him, then wins the room with one effortless reversal. |
| 8 | The Quiet Bet / Casian–Argius | Casian notices Lucan has placed five small pieces around the charter vote. He makes a private bet with Argius on how many will turn first. |
| 9 | Sabeth in Silk / Sabeth | Sabeth appears as a foreign merchant's companion, amused that Casian has walked willingly into a den of predators. |
| 10 | The Missing Necklace / Casian–Seraine | A staged theft is meant to embarrass Seraine and implicate Vale. Casian solves it by making the guilty noble expose himself in public without knowing why. |
| 11 | The King's Table / Aldren–Casian | King Aldren meets the Vale family. Casian performs harmlessness so well that the King almost forgets him—until Casian makes one dry observation that lands perfectly. |
| 12 | The Game Declared / Lucan | Lucan files a formal objection to the charter, believing he has forced Vale into months of humiliating procedure. Casian smiles because the board is finally set. |

| 38 | The Sea Map / Casian | Casian sees the newly chartered trade map and decides he wants to see the sea for himself. No enemy forces the choice. |
