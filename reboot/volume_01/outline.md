# Volume 1 — *The Gardener*
> **Kind of story:** intimate. A family, a border, a cruel man.
> **Interrupts:** ordinary life.
> **Calendar:** summer–autumn 1217. Casian is 16. Every chapter frontmatter carries `year: 1217`.
> **Status:** chapter plan, approved for drafting only after read-through.

---

## The dependency ledger

**Filled in before a single beat was written. This is the volume's spine, not its paperwork.**

### Who cannot act without him when the volume opens
Nobody. That is the point. Volume 1 is the only volume where the answer is *nobody*, and the reader must feel the difference by chapter 40.

### Who he makes dependent, in order, by name
| Who | What they stop being able to do alone | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Maeve** | read the east's accounts without asking what he wants them to mean | ch. 4 |
| **Ansel** | sell a debt without telling him whose it is | ch. 7 |
| **Corin** | decide what his own men do next | ch. 8 |
| **Kael** | run a patrol without a route from somewhere else | ch. 23 |
| **Jory** | solve a problem before reporting it | ch. 30 |
| **Calla of Fenwick** | settle a boundary dispute without referring it to Vale | ch. 25 |
| **Eleven villagers** | get their own grain back | ch. 15 |
| **Osric's people** | choose a patron | ch. 35 |
| **The eastern marches** | believe that anything is their own doing | ch. 39 |

### Who becomes *less* dependent
**Vesna. Only Vesna.** She arrives being given tasks and leaves giving herself orders — ch. 29, where she moves four families without instructions and is late and loses one of them, and does not ask permission again. She is the counter-evidence and she must visibly pay for it.

This is the volume's one honest success and it must be *harder* than every erasure in the book.

### The count
**Opening:** 0. **Closing:** 41 named people and an unnumbered marches. Chapter 39 is titled after the number, and he looks at the list for a while and does not say what he is thinking. Argius does not fill the silence.

### The cost, stated once
**Aldous dies in ch. 19** because the Hollow was not looking and neither was Casian. Not a failure of power — a failure of *attention*. He was at a market being a boy. This is the volume's only death he did not choose, and it is the reason the network exists. Nothing else in the volume is allowed to carry that weight.

## Aura payoff inventory

**Rule:** at least two per act, no more than three of the same kind.

| Act | Payoffs | Kinds |
|---|---|---|
| I | ch. 1, ch. 10 | unnoticed redirection; physical absence |
| II | ch. 15, ch. 18, ch. 21 | extraction; economic mischief; **the Council Beat** |
| III | ch. 27, ch. 32, ch. 34 | witnessed terror; the trap that isn't; staged erasure |
| IV | ch. 36, ch. 38 | aftermath-as-legend; the domain made legal |

No two erasures are allowed to look alike. Ch. 10 is a place that is not there. Ch. 15 is people who are not there. Ch. 27 is men seen and then unseen. Ch. 34 is a man taken apart while he is still talking.

## The Tolan thread

The volume's only surviving enemy, and the one piece of volume 2 that has to be planted here.

| Ch. | What the reader learns about him |
|---|---|
| 5 | He commands the raid on the Vale holding and counts the dead himself, and is quiet afterwards. Competent, not a butcher. |
| 7 | *(indirect)* He is mentioned by name among the captains Ansel sells information about — the reader registers the name a second time. |
| 10 | He owned the supply crossing that is no longer there. He investigates honestly, finds nothing, and is laughed at. He starts keeping a written record. |
| 18 | He is the only man in Thornwall who does not sell out when Osric's merchants do, and he does it for no reason he can articulate. |
| 21 | **The Council Beat.** He sees the servant's face and understands, and says nothing. |
| 22 | He works it out fully, tells no one, and keeps a horse ready. He is not a coward; he has simply concluded that the men in that room are going to die and he would prefer not to. |
| 34 | He is not in the camp when it comes apart. He is on the road, with his record, alive. |

He is never converted and never becomes Casian's man. He carries a written record of things that could not have happened into volume 2, where the Ash will believe him.

## Carry-out to Volume 2

- **Dead:** Hask Veyr, Osric (removed, unpublic), Aldous, eleven villagers (three of them — name them in ch. 39's list), two at the burned holding in ch. 5.
- **Alive and dangerous:** Tolan, who understood and was not believed and will be believed next time; Selwyn, who has written a report that is wrong in exactly the way Casian needs.
- **The Hollow exists, and it is five months old.** Four people who work for him (Maeve, Ansel, Corin, Kael), eleven villagers who owe him a rescue, one scout who gives herself orders. Sixteen people, none of them "members," no ranks, no oath, no name — *he* never names it at all. It is a habit, not an order. **It is not ancient, it is not inherited, and nothing about it predates this summer.**
- **Reputation:** the marches say *curse*, and mean luck, and will mean something worse by volume 2.
- **Seraine:** has nothing, suspects everything, and has decided to be useful rather than right.
- **The garden:** one walnut tree, planted for a man who expects to be here for all of it.

## Questions this draft must answer

1. Does Casian read as warm rather than eerie in ch. 26, where Elna is nearly taken? The chapter has to hold both: a family man frightened for four seconds *and* someone for whom fear has never been an option.
2. Is Tolan's survival set up early enough to pay off in volume 2?
3. Is forty-one the right number? It must be small enough to feel like people and large enough to be a problem.
