## Start state

- Casian Vale, 16, fourth son of Baron Edric. The Current, Argius, and a world he remembers and cannot name.
- **No network. No order. No reputation.** Volume 1 is the only volume where he is genuinely alone, and it should feel like it.
- The Keeping is a folk custom everybody practises and nobody explains.
- The Vale barony is small, warm, and quietly prosperous. Edric is a good lord who sees an ordinary son.
- Hask Veyr holds the eastern hills. Osric of Thornwall wants the road the Vales sit on.
- Seraine and Sabeth have not entered the story. Seraine arrives ch. 17, light touch only.

## Standing locks

- Casian is never threatened, never strained, never afraid. His only deaths are ones he did not prevent, and the volume allows exactly one (Aldous, ch. 19).
- Nobody learns the old world. Argius remembers; Argius does not speak of it beyond what is needed.
- The mask is pleasure, not pressure. Ch. 6 and ch. 31 must both carry this.
- No modern register outside Casian's private thought and Argius's voice.
- Vesna is not a romance lane. Seraine is, and in volume 1 it is *politics and sharp company* — she is nineteen and he is sixteen and the volume must not pretend otherwise.
- The Hollow is feared from its first appearance (ch. 7–8) and is described as a curse before it is described as anything else.

  - Irreversible: **the family is committed to standing in the open.** Casian's answer to Argius — *"Then I'd better be worth it"* — is the volume's thesis in six words.
- **ch_032 *The Siege That Wasn't* (Tolan).** Two hundred men come over the drove and the ford sells them supper; Hollis's wife chose to stay and charge two pennings a cup after being told it was hers to decide. At dawn, gates open, the Baron of Vale at a table, one free man with a weapon on the wall and he looks bored. **Tolan works out the whole design in four minutes and cannot stop it:** burn it, take the baron, stand in the road, or eat — and eating is the only move that doesn't lose. A levy man is flogged in the yard and the yard watches it over bread. **Thirty-one men walk away before dark.** Tolan's verdict: *"The trap was never the trap."* Elna carries the water round all day and learns the column by its boots; Maren tells her to watch the flogging properly.
  - **Ends on Casian standing at the gate for an hour not to watch the column but to be seen** — Tolan crosses the yard toward him. Hand-off to ch. 33.
  - Deviations from the outline, deliberate: **ch. 26 is Elna's POV** (the outline had Hask), and **ch. 32 is Tolan's** with two family sections. Both were kept because the aura and the siege read better as testimony than as explanation.

- **ch_033 *The Boy at the Gate* (Casian).** The tally-room. Hask works out within ten minutes that the boy from the kitchen is the thing he has been hunting and still cannot make him into an enemy. Casian asks for one thing — **that Hask go home tonight and answer at Harven** — and offers him a way out that would actually have worked. Hask's refusal is the volume's real hinge, and it is not stupid: *"You're not going to be anything, you know… a man of your sort does not last past his patron."* He tells Casian where he will camp (the long field at Rye, for the water) because he has decided the boy is harmless. Casian's warning is stated plainly and taken for a boy's politeness: *"it'll come a piece at a time, and it'll take about eleven days, and at the end of it there'll be a horse standing somewhere with nobody with it."*
  - **Tolan confronts him in the yard and gets nothing but the truth:** *"if he goes home tonight he lives… and eleven of them will not, and it will be my fault, and I'll not be able to write it in the book."* Tolan says he will go on writing it down. Casian: *"one of us is going to be wrong about how this ends, and I'd rather it was me."*
  - Edric, on being told the boy gave Hask a way out and was not heard: *"I'd have done the same at your age and I'd have got the same answer."* Ends on the goose and "...I'll be there" — the last fully ordinary line before the volume's kill.
- **ch_034 *In Stages* (Hask).** Eleven days in the long field at Rye, structured by day. Ninety-one levy men go home; Thornwall's letters turn into a clerk's letter; Berrin leaves with eleven men on the seventh; the Harrowmere corn contract is already sold to Corran and Son (bought in February, innocently, by a factor who did not know); Ferrin goes to Harven with the deed and writes back four lines resigning; **three horses go, with halters unbuckled and laid straight** — the ch. 27 signature, and Hask recognises it. Weyl leaves to his face. Only **nine men answer the summons of four hundred**, and the last letter is from a woman in a village he roofed a chapel for: *"if that is what the Vale does to two hundred men, then I would rather be at the mill than in the field."*
  - **The tent.** Casian comes in alone on the eleventh night and sits by the fire. The whole mechanism is stated and it is ordinary: *"I've never once done a thing to a man that he didn't do to himself when he'd had a fortnight of being frightened on my account."* Casian's accusation is the one that lands — **Hask could have taken the county in October and was too careful.** Ends: flap tied from the inside, lamp still burning, two cups, one full, no man. A horse found three days later at the crossing below the Veyr, **saddle unbuckled and girthed folded**, nobody with it.
  - **Lock respected:** Casian neither strains nor threatens; he answers every question honestly and is, in the tent, almost kind. **Hask's death is an absence, not a killing** — and the volume will let the county argue about it forever.
- **ch_035 *Thornwall Falls Quietly* (Osric).** April. The Count's clerk Orlan arrives with Seraine's thirty-one-page report, which accuses nobody and simply puts 412 households on one side of a page and the sum that should be in a strongbox on the other. **Rennick turns on Osric first** and does it well. **Wex**, a copying clerk of twenty-three paid eleven marks a year, recognises a forged seal and a forged hand because it is his whole trade, burns his copy, is sick twice, and goes to the Count's man — *"it'll be a thing a baron can't take off you."* Nobody will ever put his name on a stone.
  - **Casian comes to Thornwall on the 19th and tells Osric the entire truth** — the eleven out of the barn, Bready's, the nineteen off the road (alive, at work, eight have written home), Hask's camp — knowing it cannot be used, because a baron caught with 400 marks under his floor saying "a gardener of sixteen did it" will be written in the margin as *unwell*. **"To be left alone. You gave me a coin for it. It's still the answer."**
  - Osric's removal is quiet: stripped, lands taken, a house and a pension near his wife's people. Rennick's parting line — *"a curse can't be reasoned with and a boy can"* — is the seed of ch. 37 and the volume's next danger.
- **ch_036 *The Warden's Report* (Selwyn).** Selwyn of the Ash, 63, no authority but a pen, rides the marches in late April and May. **Eleven crossings, not four; seven in repair; the newest two or three years old and the work of a trained mason.** Watt genuinely believes there are four gates. He takes testimony from Orm, Berrin, the Ord woman, Vesna, and Tolan, and it is the best book of testimony of his life. In the garden at Vale he asks the only question that matters — *"are you frightened of anything?"* — and gets *"no,"* and then the boy's real fear: **being believed and being wrong.**
  - He writes forty pages, gets every fact right to the day, and **writes a wrong conclusion on purpose**: an observance older than the Ash has woken. His reasoning is not cowardice and he says so out loud: a heresy report gets a village burned; a curse report gets filed. *"Nobody burns a village over a curse."*
  - Tolan signs as to the facts only: *"The opinion is the Warden's… who has written everything down and does not know what it was either."*
  - **This is the document that will define the Hollow in volume 2.** It is in a box, with a wrong answer at the bottom of it.

## Open threads after ch. 36

- **Rennick** is in Harven giving evidence and believes a boy can be reasoned with.
- Casian still owes Vesna the promise from ch. 29: **say aloud what you are for, where people can hear it** — ch. 38.


- **ch_037 *A Dance of Facts* (Seraine).** Harven, June. The hearing is dull on purpose and Edric's honesty is what damns him: he doesn't know what happened and sounding like a man covering for it is worse than lying. **Marshal Vane** is courteous and it is a warning — he tells Seraine to her face that her father asked him to be careful of her. His closing remark to the Count is the volume's real alarm: whatever is happening in the east *"is not a matter of a levy, and in ten years it will not be a small thing, and the crown has no man in that country and no means of putting one there."* **Rennick's offer to her** — *"a boy like that can be reasoned with"* — is the seed of volume 2's approach.
  - Seraine's cloister scene with Casian: she lays out everything she knows, correctly, and it amounts to nothing; he answers honestly inside his limits and does not perform. Her decision is stated plainly and it is her own: she will go on writing the quarterly reports and write them so that they protect four thousand people. Her crossed-out line ("I will not write another report that does not say that") is a vow, not a plot.
- **ch_038 *The First Boundary* (Casian).** Corin writes **eleven articles**, and Ivet is the reason they're readable (*"Say what it is and then shut up"*). They are read from a barrel at the June fair in front of 411 people by a boy whose voice breaks twice. Article ten is Corin's and the one that matters: **the mill will not take a matter to a lawyer, will not send for a lord, and will not ask any man to swear an oath.** Casian keeps Vesna's March promise and says aloud what he is for — and then says the uncomfortable half before anyone else can: he is sixteen, he does not hold the county, and *"if you'd rather have a baron and a lawyer, say so at the fair and I'll put the book on the barrel and walk home."*
  - **Edric signs**, knowingly reducing his own office: *"it is not the office I inherited, and I'd like you to understand that I know it, and that I'm signing anyway."*
  - Seraine rides nineteen miles to watch a Tuesday: nine cases, no oaths, one man caught by his own answer, a boy of nine fined a penny.
  - Argius's warning, and Casian's best line of the volume: *"that's the whole of the danger, isn't it. Not the taking. The being liked while you take."*
- **ch_039 *Forty Names* (Maeve).** **The dependency count lands at 41** — Maeve has been keeping the list since 4 March, in the back of the barley book, crossing nobody out but Vesna. Two entries do the work: *Edric Vale — asks his fourth son before he signs a thing, and does it openly, and has told me so* and *Casian Vale — decides.*
  - Casian's response is institutional, not emotional: **three keepers of the book, two hearers of a case, the race at Fenwick in Calla's name, a second person in the surgery** (Nessa chose her and told him rather than asking), and **the list read aloud at the June fair every year whatever the number is.** He writes a page in the front of the book beginning *"I have made a county that cannot lose me, which is not the same as one that does not need me."*
  - Nessa makes him write *the surgery has two people in it* on the money page, not in the shame list.
- **ch_040 *Three Buds* (Casian).** The walnut planted 18 October, nine feet, eleven marks, bought *because* the nurseryman said it wouldn't take. A family dinner that is deliberately and completely ordinary — the drainage story again, Brennan's barrel, Emric asleep on Gareth, Elna asking what happened to the nine men who came in February and getting an answer she doesn't like. **Argius's last speech** names the four things that will outlive him (millrace, road, surgery, the list) and says the thing that closes the volume's arc: *"I said in the spring that you would be here for all of it. I have never once been wrong about that part."*
  - **Three buds on the scion in October**, which is the wrong season and which Maren notices first. Then the gate, which somebody has shut. And the world arriving on purpose: **Seraine's letter** — a spring commission, a clerk of her own, and *"I have told you first."* Casian is pleased. That pleasure is the last note of volume 1.
