## Start state

- Casian Vale, 16, fourth son of Baron Edric. The Current, Argius, and a world he remembers and cannot name.
- **No network. No order. No reputation.** Volume 1 is the only volume where he is genuinely alone, and it should feel like it.
- The Keeping is a folk custom everybody practises and nobody explains.
- The Vale barony is small, warm, and quietly prosperous. Edric is a good lord who sees an ordinary son.
- Hask Veyr holds the eastern hills. Osric of Thornwall wants the road the Vales sit on.
- Seraine and Sabeth have not entered the story. Seraine arrives ch. 17, light touch only.

## Standing locks

- Casian is never threatened, never strained, never afraid. His only deaths are ones he did not prevent, and the volume allows exactly one (Aldous, ch. 19).
- Nobody learns the old world. Argius remembers; Argius does not speak of it beyond what is needed.
- The mask is pleasure, not pressure. Ch. 6 and ch. 31 must both carry this.
- No modern register outside Casian's private thought and Argius's voice.
- Vesna is not a romance lane. Seraine is, and in volume 1 it is *politics and sharp company* — she is nineteen and he is sixteen and the volume must not pretend otherwise.
- The Hollow is feared from its first appearance (ch. 7–8) and is described as a curse before it is described as anything else.

## Continuity log

- **ch_001 *The Bench* (Casian).** Morning in the walled garden; roses cut back hard; Elna (12) on the bench. A scout on the ridge, one and a quarter miles out, sent from Hask's country the long way round — Casian turns the horse and the man goes home without ever knowing, from a bench, without standing up. Oswin (71) says the most suspicious thing about him is how pleasant he is. Supper, twelve at the table, Emric (1) beating a spoon. **New:** Edric reveals four riders "going west, not stopping" three days back on the Ashgrove road — the first thread of the ch. 5 burn. Edric's line to hold: *"You're the only one of them that doesn't want something from me. It's restful and it worries me."* After supper Casian walks the ridge and stands where the scout sat, looking down at his own house the way a stranger would. Ends on the walnut and Argius's line: *"You will be here for all of it."*
  - Irreversible: nothing yet, and that is deliberate. No one in the house knows. No name has been spoken aloud that matters.
  - Seeded: the Ashgrove road, four riders, Osric unnamed, Hask unnamed, the walnut, the east wall needing stone before the wet.
- **ch_002 *Forty Years* (Casian).** The graft takes in four days. The water channel and its four leather gates. Maren watches him open the last one and says nothing — *"the flat neutral interest she used for the water channel"* is her whole character. Breakfast. Hob the mason mentions building work at Thornwall paid for out of a purse too full. Gareth reports on Hask Veyr: a hundred men, no title, a slate roof, bought land and kept the sellers — *"he's going to buy a friend first."* Oswin asks what he will do when the next bad man comes. Argius asks what it actually retains: a street that sloped, a smell, seven words with no grammar, a number whose purpose is gone. Ends on the walnut and *"I will."*
  - Irreversible: Argius's archive is confirmed as fragments, not records — there is no history to recover.
  - Seeded: Hask named for the first time. Thornwall money with no obvious source.
- **ch_003 *What the Marches Keep* (Casian).** Fenwick, the moved boundary stone, Reed and Tolsey both honest and both wrong. Casian settles it without a verdict and both men leave believing they won. Calls out that a stone was lifted and set down two or three seasons ago, not walked. The Keeping explained as custom: nobody knows why, the Ash has given up on it, and the fear is real and never discussed. A frightened farmer, oxen that would not pass the Fenwick stone. Calla: *"Don't be charming at me, it's the least interesting thing about you."* He gives her cuttings. Edric's report scene: *"I want you to grow up in a country that isn't frightened of its own fields."*
  - Irreversible: **someone moved a Keeping stone two seasons before the story and nobody saw it.** Open thread, deliberately unexplained.
  - Seeded: Calla now watching him. Edric has decided which questions he can afford.
- **ch_004 *The Ledger at the Mill* (Maeve).** The mill's paper is held by Rennick of Thornwall, 412 marks, 131 of it fraud. Casian reads the town — eleven shops on one street carrying paper from the same factor, and Osric's steward riding a horse bred in Hask's hills. He buys the paper at full price in coin in front of a clerk, on purpose, so that Rennick has money and no story. Offers Maeve a loan at no interest for six years and asks for one thing: the book, and to be told what the numbers mean. Oswin's warning: *"It concerns you. Every time, in the small hours, in a room with no one in it."* Edric notices, and says nothing, and starts watching.
  - **Dependency count: 1 (Maeve).**
  - Irreversible: he has spent most of his capital and has a creditor's claim on a miller, which is the first thing he owns that can be taken from him.
  - Seeded: Osric's steward, the eleven shops, Rennick, Brennan's loan (Brennan will want to know eventually).
- **ch_005 *Ash on the Road* (Family).** Mirra Cott (50) and Jon (19) killed at the Ashgrove holding. Tolan leads, counts the dead himself, and now carries a number that stands at thirty-one since he started counting six years ago. Edric: law is slow and it is what we have. Maren privately tells Casian she has known for twenty years that the letters are worth nothing: *"do it so that I am never sat in a kitchen being told about it by a man with a hat in his hands."* Casian identifies the fifth road — a drove over the Common that carries goods, not men.
  - Irreversible: **Mirra and Jon are dead.** Casian's founding reason is now on the page. Tolan is a named, thinking, reluctant antagonist.
- **ch_006 *The Easy Brother* (Casian).** The Harrowmere fair. Wool now goes east, over the cut, up into the hills. Hask is "polite" and remembers his stable boys' names — Casian dislikes that he sees himself in it. Meets Rennick, who is cheerful and unafraid and warns him off lightly. Confirms the drove road as a fifth route. Writes it down: *anything I know about, I can do — the whole difficulty is the knowing.*
  - Irreversible: Rennick has now seen his face and been pleasant to him.
- **ch_007 *The Fence at Harrowmere* (Casian).** Ansel Trask, 60, the fence of the eastern road, whose paper has been quietly taken from him over two years — he has become a clerk and no one has noticed. Casian tells him so. Eleven names, one of them Osric four years old, one Rennick. Ansel's condition: the truth about what he was. Casian's realisation afterwards: *he let me win.*
  - **Dependency count: 3 (Maeve, Ansel, and a promise to a miller).**
  - Irreversible: eleven names exist in Casian's hand only, and Ansel has burned his own deniability.
- **ch_008 *The Man Who Waits* (Corin).** Corin Ahl, 35 years an armsman, given the first task in his life that uses the whole of him: a wagon down the Ashgrove road as bait, reported to the baron rather than to Casian. Finds a cold fire square on the drove — nine men, ten nights, sited properly. Concludes the enemy has a soldier working for a man who isn't one. Jory covers for him. Edric's line: *"Is he right?"*
  - Irreversible: **the Hollow now has a second working part and its first real risk.** Corin has written a private note asking why a boy of sixteen can see what he cannot.
- **ch_009 *What Osric Wants* (Osric).** The top of the enemy ladder on the page. Osric, 55, careful for thirty years, a genuinely fair landlord who gets down off his horse to settle a boundary. He is not stupid: he works out that paying the fraudulent 131 marks means the boy read the book. He proposes the eastern strip as eleven parcels of nobody's land, and writes to Hask *without naming him*, to the quiet man on the grey horse. Seraine Varyn's inspection is announced. Hask's reply arrives in nine days and offers Osric his own ambition back, and Osric burns it and understands he is being priced.
  - Irreversible: **Osric has committed and Hask has his measure.**
- **ch_010 *The Empty Ford* (Casian–Tolan).** Casian takes out the causeway on the Veyr — not the men, the crossing. He does it from the garden bench with a cup in his hand, and it rains, and the stone goes. Four drivers walk home with a story about a mist. Tolan investigates for two days, drags the channel, interviews all four separately, and works out that a story which does not improve in the telling is a story somebody has thought about. He is laughed at in the hall and writes it down in a book titled *Things that should not have been so.*
  - Aura payoff #2: **a place that isn't there.**
  - Irreversible: Hask has a winter with a hundred men and no grain. **Tolan now has a written record.**
- **ch_011 *The Girl Who Walks in the Dark* (Vesna).** Vesna Orrow, 19, uplander, hired by Corin. Nine runs. On the seventh she hears two men and a horse going north on the moor road — toward a place, not a raid. Then she walks the cut and finds **four gates with no gates in them**, a line of crossings built into an old wall above the line where any road would go, weathered as old as the wall itself. She asks what she is part of and is told the truth. She asks for Fenwick to be told about her, and for it to be said by Casian and not by her.
  - Irreversible: **the four gates are the second unexplained old-country thread** (with the moved Fenwick stone). She is in.
- **ch_012 *The Shape of It* (Casian).** The old dairy. Five people around a map for two hours. Maeve, Corin, Vesna, Ansel and Casian. Money argument: two riders cost twenty marks a quarter, Maeve lends nine, Ansel four, and Casian refuses any more lending because *the day it costs me more than it costs you is the day you're entitled to a vote.* Ansel's point that wins the evening: the danger is not the men coming out of the hills, it is the forty men already in the county who owe somebody outside it. Casian says the thing out loud once — that he has brought a map, a purse and a bad idea, and everything else was built by them. Then: **Hask gets one move**, so every concealed friend has to come into the open.
  - Dependency count: **14**.
  - Irreversible: the thing has a shape and five people know it has a shape. Elna reports a grey-coated man who counted eleven at table in the spring. Edric says he will not ask, and that he will know.
- **ch_013 *Teeth* (Hask).** Hask's own chapter. The arithmetic of a hundred and six men and eighty horses and no bread. Three options, two of them bad. He will not be at Lowmere because he wants to be able to say truthfully that he was not there: *"I'll not be at Lowmere because I want to be able to say, truthfully, in front of a Warden, that I was not at Lowmere."* Tolan asks for twenty men on the barn; he gets four. Eleven taken (Ferrin the headman, a boy of fifteen, a girl of nine, seven others), two barns burned, forty minutes. Hask rides up to the barn alone on the Sunday and looks in through the door and cannot say why.
  - Irreversible: **eleven people are in a barn and Hask has committed.** Tolan has asked for what he wanted in writing.
- **ch_014 *The Price of Patience* (Corin).** Vale's paralysis: five letters, one burned, one to the Bishop that cost Edric something. Gareth's fury — *"If it can't be used, what's it for?"* Vesna confronts Corin in the yard. Corin admits he has decided to follow a thing that knows rather than be correct and slow. Casian tells Corin plainly that he is not going in until Wednesday, and why: eleven people are the price of getting every concealed friend into the light. Corin: *"if those eleven come home, I'll follow you into whatever's after, and if they don't, I'll be a man who sat in a guardroom and let a boy of sixteen explain arithmetic to me."*
  - Irreversible: **Corin has chosen, and knows what he chose.**

Vesna walks the eleven out at the pace of a nine-year-old. Ferrin's POV inside the barn: he had rehearsed what he would do when they came to move him, and did not need to do any of it. Corin and Watt lay out four men with cloaks over their faces and Watt asks the question. Corin reports to Edric and refuses to say how; Edric says *"It was my son"* and chooses not to ask. Maren asks only whether the girl was warm and whether it was quick.
  - **Dependency count: 16** (5 + eleven rescued).
  - Irreversible: Edric knows and has decided to let it continue. Maren knows and has decided to ask only the questions she can live with.
- **ch_016 *What Maeve Was Reading* (Maeve).** Three nights with the mill book. The levy: nine marks a quarter, forty-one men, two years, 738 marks, going out as "sundries" and over the cut. Thirty of the thirty-two she can name have a grudge against the Vales. It is not a levy, it is a payroll. She gets a paper out of Widow Rill's son, countersigned by Rennick and signed by Osric's steward Weyl. Her warning at the door: whatever he builds, the thirty men with grudges are in it and nobody has asked them.
  - Irreversible: **there is now written evidence that could hang a baron.** Maeve has made herself a person who can be killed for it.
- **ch_017 *The Old Complaint* (Seraine).** Seraine Varyn, 19, arrives after six days in the county records at Harrowmere. She is good at noticing when a man tells her a thing that is true and is not the thing that matters. Three findings: the county has been getting *paid*, not rich; eleven recognisances lodged by one clerk for an unnamed patron; and the levy is not a levy but a wage. Edric is honest and ineffective and knows it. Calla gives her the barn at Fenwick. She writes to her father and tells him not to send anyone yet. Her error: she reads the Vales as bystanders who noticed, and does not see that the fourth son is the thing she is looking for.
  - Irreversible: **the crown now has a report that names Thornwall.** The clock on Osric is running.
- **ch_018 *A Fourth Son's Errand* (Casian).** Two days in Thornwall being harmless by choice, with three rehearsed versions of his errand. Finds Rennick's lean-to bolted from the road and a boy from the north living in the loft since September — Osric's people are taking delivery of people, which means Lowmere was not the whole of it. Sees the queue outside the factor's house: Osric is buying the town with the levy. Hears Tolan in the Bell: *"the man who pays me has never once asked me to do a thing he wouldn't have been able to say in his own hall, and that has been gone since September."* Ends by making a fool of himself at Rennick's own counter, and Argius tells him plainly that this was pleasure, not mask.
  - Irreversible: **Casian now knows people are being traded, not just raided.**
- **ch_019 *Nothing Happened at Ashgrove* (Casian).** Nine men come to burn the Vale granary. Casian could take them on the road at four in the morning and does not, because he wants a witness; he puts them in a lane. Eight die, one goes home. **Aldous is knifed in the lane in the ninety seconds before Casian acts** — he went out because a thing was happening beside his house. He works all night on other people, asks after a boy's arm, tells Nessa to leave his wife asleep till light, and dies at dawn. Nessa takes his tools. Corin's rule: *don't make a lesson of a thing you can stop.* Maren asks him for the truth and gets it.
  - **This is the volume's cost. It is not a failure of power; it is a failure of attention and of wanting a story.**
- **ch_020 *I Was Not Looking* (Casian).** The funeral; four hundred people, three hundred of whom are not Vale's. The meeting where the network's true purpose is said aloud for the first time: *a thing that has nowhere in this county I'm not* — not to kill anyone, but so that a thing starting in the east is known before it is near a house. Ansel says it back. Nessa's condition: keep the surgery open, train a second person, and account for every penny in a book she can see. Nessa's insight that changes the meeting: they need a door that isn't the Vale. Maeve: *"there's houses in this county that every man will walk into, and one of them is a mill."* Also: money is nearly gone, and Casian proposes a real, dull, named carrying business as cover and wage.
  - Dependency count: **18**.
  - Irreversible: **the network has a stated purpose, a dullness planned, a new door, and a stated end-state.** Edric's warning, the series thesis: *"you'll be dangerous because you'll be needed, and there's no wall in the world for that."*
- **ch_021 *The Council in Osric's Hall* (Hask). THE COUNCIL BEAT.** Five men at a table: Osric, Weyl, Rennick, Hask, Tolan. The room is competent. Rennick proposes three things: a deed against the eastern strip; forged letters dated '16 giving *the valley* to the Count; and — the worst — a report to Warden Selwyn that the eastern marches harbour the old thing, so the Church does the work and the Count spends the spring looking at a heresy instead of at Thornwall. Hask refuses to touch the plan he doesn't understand. Osric asks the room what the thing wants. **The boy with the jug answers: "To be left alone."** Osric laughs, gives him a coin, sends him to bed. Tolan is the only one who sees. Then the realisation in stages: no jug in that kitchen, no cellarer, a carter paid four marks to let a boy ride up. They find nothing.
  - Irreversible: **the plan for the spring is set, and it is to hand the Vales to a Church over a thing that is true.** Casian has heard the whole of it and taken a coin for his trouble.
- **ch_022 *What Tolan Did Not Say* (Tolan).** Tolan's third book. He goes back to the ford and finds an eight-horse standing place with no fire and a woman's boot-heel and a linen bandage — the party came down off the cut. Works out there is one thing behind all of it and it is not a warlord. Gives the second book to Ferrin for safekeeping. Rides to Fenwick and asks a woman in a field, hat in hand, whether anyone has been asking questions — which is him putting a hand out to Casian without knowing it. Writes it all down: *"I asked for twenty men on that barn in October, and for the yard to be sealed on the nineteenth, and both times I was told no by men who were certain."* Buys a horse in September. Tells Osric plainly that the carefulness is what got him here.
  - Irreversible: **Tolan is out of the room in all but body, and the written record now exists.**
- **ch_023 *The Ground Prepares Itself* (Corin).** Corran and Son opens (1 Dec) — a real, dull, licensed carrying business, nine marks in its first month, deliberately unprofitable-looking. Maeve's lesson: *a man keeps a business book and writes his sins on the same page as his barley.* Corin makes eleven places in the county worth being — repaired folds with a gap in the wall a man can lie in. Kael joins (9 Dec), the second rider. **Vesna walks all nineteen miles of the cut and finds eleven gates, not four — and the newest has been mended two or three years ago by someone who is not Casian, not Hask, not the Church.** Casian is told to leave it alone.
  - Dependency count: **20**.
  - Irreversible: **there is a third party maintaining the old crossings.** Edric hears of the finding against the eastern strip and decides to go to Harven in the spring.
- **ch_024 *Osric's Courtesy* (Seraine).** Three days at Thornwall. She counts four lies and can prove none. A grey hill horse in the yard whose master calls himself a farmer. Hask tells her plainly to ask *the man who took the eleven out of the barn*, and means all of it — the one flat note she does not hear. Rennick cheerfully tells her the levy is "on the sundries" and does not know he has said anything. She finds the east range room with four cups and a jug and one cup unused, and no council in the hall. She works out the levy-and-debt loop from a queue in a street.
  - Irreversible: **Seraine knows there was a secret council and that Osric wants to know which road she takes.** The Count's clock is running.


- **ch_025 *The Eastern Strip* (Casian).** The finding at Harven is *true and used as a lever*. He buys **debts, not land** — six of eleven tenants, 460 marks at nothing in the hundred, held and not entered. **Brennan** gives 200 marks and asks to be in a thing. **Maeve** raises 400 from four men at six and a half and lends at five. The **millrace and water right** bought for eleven marks: *"A paper is a paper. A mill with water in it is a fact."* **Hew Ord**, turned off Vale land in the bad winter, is told the truth in nine sentences and believes him only because he admits it isn't personal. The race is **dug in daylight in front of forty-one people** off a notice read out in church — Corin's idea, and the best thing he does all year. **Calla** agrees to hold two parcels on three conditions: tell her everything in the book, put the mill in the village's name, and answer "I'm not going to answer that" instead of something clever.
  - Dependency count: **27** — and *four thousand people in this county I have spent five months making decisions for without once asking any of them.*
  - Irreversible: **the county can see him now.** A ditch dug by notice in front of witnesses is not a thing that can be undone.
- **ch_026 *A Wolf in the Lane* (Elna).** 11 Feb. Nine men take Bradey's holding above the mill. **Elna (12)**, sent down with a basket, does the thing her mother told her in November: water, stone, count. She hears the count fail at twenty, hears two men three feet away decide a basket isn't a girl, then **runs to the mill instead of home** and gets Maeve to ring the bell. Gareth arrives in nine minutes with fourteen men and takes seven without a charge. Casian looks at the seven for four seconds, sees Thornwall behind them, spends an hour in the garden, and then does the thing that is not the law: **lets all nine go home in the morning with bread and their boots**, because *"the last time I made it so that nine men couldn't do a thing. This time I'm going to make it so that four hundred men won't."*
  - Irreversible: **Elna is in the story as a person who did something.** The barony's law was not used and Gareth knows it and cannot say why.
  - Coda: the story mutates to *a book of names*, *bread baked over a fire he lit with his hand*; Casian writes the versions in the back of the book under *What they say*. Rennick: *"It's a trick and I'll find it."*
- **ch_027 *Not There* (Tolan).** The aura payoff. Berrin, a captain of nine years, cannot tell the story without being laughed at. 22 Feb: a woodward sees **eight men in a lane and then no men** — and his dog will not stand up. 24 Feb: twenty-three ride down the Ashgrove road in daylight; six men stand at the ford with no weapons; Berrin turns his head and **nineteen men are off the road and nineteen saddles are unbuckled and laid straight.** Four come home. On the 26th the horses come up the road on their own, led in by a boy of fourteen, saddles folded — and Hask forbids anybody to ride them. Tolan checks his horse, then saddles it, and rides for **Harrowmere to read Warden Selwyn's letters**, then Harven. His last word to Hask: *"I have served three men in twenty years and not one of them would have left the horses."*
  - Irreversible: **the eastern hills now contain a thing no company will go up against**, and Tolan has left the valley. Hask's authority is broken in his own hall.
- **ch_028 *What Seraine Saw* (Seraine).** 3 March. She walks the road for a day and finds nothing a story can be built on: no blood, no tracks, no dropped tack, nineteen saddles unbuckled. Nib's testimony: *"There wasn't the sound of a man shifting his feet."* Sennet's aunt: nine men came back and one gave her grandson a penny. **Gareth**, grey-faced, tells her it was a person and that his father knows and will not say, and that he will not be the son who says it first. She writes an eleven-page report — levy, queue, table, the Baron of Vale to be heard at Harven — and **crosses the true thing out of it**, because out loud it sounds like a duke's daughter frightened by a county, and putting a true thing where it can discredit three true things is how Thornwall wins. She sends it instead **in a sealed letter to her father alone**, four pages, naming what she saw. The reply is five lines from his clerk: the Duke will speak to her in person; stay in the east until Harven; **say nothing to the Royal Marshal, who is travelling in that country this month.**
  - Irreversible: **one man in the kingdom now knows there is a person in the east doing this on purpose** — and someone in the crown's own apparatus is already moving through the county.
  - Locked for ch. 29+: she has promised Osric his name goes in the report, and it does. Integrity is her method, not her weakness.
